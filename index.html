U.S. Used-Car Buyer's Guide & FAQ (All 50 States + D.C.)

Free Buyer Guide

Buying a used Honda, Toyota, Ford, Chevy, or SUV from Facebook Marketplace, Craigslist, OfferUp, or an independent dealer lot? Run these checks before meeting the seller.

Why follow the 15 → 5 → 3 → 1 U.S. buyer game plan instead of buying reports upfront?

Traditional history reports cost $40–$45 each — checking 5 listings can burn $200+ before you even find a clean car. Screening 10–15 listings free with AI removes obvious traps first, so you only spend $8.99 on your Top 5 NHTSA VIN checks and pay a mechanic for a Pre-Purchase Inspection (PPI) on just your 1 Core Winner.

How do I spot a used car scam on Facebook Marketplace or Craigslist?

Paste the listing description and seller replies into Step 1 above. Watch out for "bill of sale only" or missing titles, third-party curbstoning ("selling for a friend"), urgent "cash today / leaving town" pressure, Zelle or gift-card deposit requests, and "easy fix" check-engine excuses.

What does an official U.S. NHTSA VIN check show?

Step 2 queries official U.S. Department of Transportation (NHTSA) public APIs to decode the 17-digit VIN, verify model year, trim, body style, engine, and plant, and display open safety recalls plus U.S. owner complaint totals.

Does a clean listing or NHTSA recall check replace a mechanic inspection (PPI)?

No. Always match the dashboard VIN to the driver door-jamb sticker and paper title in the seller's legal name, take a highway and city test drive, and have an independent mechanic run a lift and OBD-II inspection before signing.

Is there any monthly subscription or recurring billing?

No. You get 4 free AI red-flag scans every day plus 1 starter NHTSA VIN credit at $0. Optional credit bundles like the $8.99 5-Car Pass are strictly one-time purchases covered by our 7-Day Refund Policy.
